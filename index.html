The rules

Simple & Convenient: the idea in three lines

Two sides, white and black, take turns moving one piece at a time. White always moves first.

01

Set up the board

Place the board so each player has a light square at the bottom right corner. Queens start on their own colour.

02

Move and capture

Pieces capture by landing on an opponent’s piece, which is removed from the board.

03

Trap the king

When a king is attacked and cannot escape, it is checkmate and the round is over.

Special moves

Three moves worth knowing

They look unusual at first, but each one follows a clear rule.

Castling

The king moves two squares toward a rook, and the rook jumps to the other side. It protects the king and wakes up the rook.

Promotion

A pawn that reaches the far edge of the board becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight.

En passant

A special pawn capture available for one turn right after an enemy pawn advances two squares beside it.

Checkmate

How a round ends

Check is a threat to the king. Checkmate is a threat with no escape.

The back rank pattern

Here the black king is blocked by its own three pawns. The white rook attacks along the first row, and there is nowhere to hide or capture.

This is one of the first checkmates people learn, and a good reminder to give the king some room.

History

Learn More Online: a pastime over 1,400 years old

A short timeline of one of the world’s oldest strategy pastimes.

Around the 6th century

An early form called chaturanga appeared in India, with pieces based on armies.

Persia and beyond

It became shatranj and travelled through the Arab world and into Europe.

Late 1400s

The queen and bishop gained their long-range moves, creating the faster modern form.

1886

The first official world championship match was played, between Wilhelm Steinitz and Johannes Zukertort.

Today

People play over the board, by post, and on screens, in homes and clubs around the world.

FAQ

Quick answers

Find useful information in one place.

Do I need to be good at maths?

No. Chess is about patterns, planning and careful looking, not calculation.

Who moves first?

White always moves first, then the players take turns.

What is a draw?

A draw means nobody wins. It can happen by agreement, by stalemate, or when neither side can force checkmate.

How long does a round take?

Anything from a few minutes to several hours, depending on the pace you both choose.
